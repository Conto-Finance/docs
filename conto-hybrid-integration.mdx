description: "Use Conto as the spend-management control plane while your app keeps execution and settlement."

## Summary

Use the hybrid model when your app already owns the workflow and payment path, but you want Conto to enforce spend policy before money moves.

In this model:

- your app decides when a paid action is about to happen
- Conto approves, denies, or gates the payment
- your app executes the settlement
- your app records the final spend back to Conto

## How hybrid differs from managed execution

In a managed-execution setup, Conto both authorizes and executes the payment from a Conto-managed wallet.

In a hybrid setup, your app still executes the payment. Conto acts as the control plane for policy, approvals, and auditability.

The key rule is simple:

Conto should make the authorization decision before your app executes the payment.

If your app settles first and reports later, Conto becomes an analytics mirror instead of a real spend-control layer.

## When to use this model

Choose the hybrid model when:

- your app already has its own treasury or payment executor
- paid events are embedded inside a larger workflow
- you need budget checks before API calls, machine payments, or provider settlements
- you want Conto controls without rebuilding your runtime around Conto-managed execution

This pattern is a strong fit for AI products, agents, marketplaces, and apps that use `x402`, `MPP`, or similar machine-payment rails.

## Responsibility split

### Your app owns

- user experience
- workflow and routing logic
- provider selection and fallback behavior
- execution timing
- the actual settlement call
- application-specific state

### Conto owns

- spend policies
- approval thresholds
- allowlists and counterparties
- budget controls
- runtime authorization
- audit visibility
- spend analytics

## Recommended integration flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Store user-facing controls in your app">
    Keep your own product settings and sync only the Conto-compatible subset into Conto.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provision a Conto agent for each managed actor">
    Create one agent per user, workspace, bot, or customer environment that needs isolated limits and audit history.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create and protect runtime credentials">
    Create a runtime credential for that agent, encrypt it at rest, and keep it server-side only.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sync policy objects into Conto">
    Translate your product settings into Conto policies whenever limits, allowlists, or approval rules change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Call Conto before settlement">
    Send the payment context to Conto runtime authorization before your app executes the paid action.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute payment in your own runtime">
    If the payment is approved, let your existing executor, treasury, or protocol adapter handle settlement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record settled spend back to Conto">
    Send the final amount, recipient, and transaction or payment reference back to Conto for audit and analytics.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Common policy mappings

| Product control | Conto primitive |
| --- | --- |
| Max spend per action | `SPEND_LIMIT` |
| Budget per session or run | `BUDGET_ALLOCATION` |
| Allowed providers or vendors | `COUNTERPARTY` or `WHITELIST` |
| Human review threshold | `APPROVAL_THRESHOLD` |
| Time-based restrictions | Time or blackout-related policy types |

## Verification checklist

Use this checklist after setup:

- the SDK is installed and importable
- organization credentials are configured server-side
- each managed actor gets a dedicated Conto binding
- runtime credentials are encrypted at rest
- policy changes update Conto objects
- pre-authorization happens before settlement
- denied payments never reach the execution layer
- successful payments are recorded back to Conto
- your product surfaces workspace status and recent runtime events

## Why this model works

The hybrid model lets you add strong spend controls without giving up your existing runtime.

You keep your app's orchestration and execution flow, while Conto adds approval logic, limits, and auditability at the point where payment decisions matter most.
